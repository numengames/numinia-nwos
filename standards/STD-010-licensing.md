# STD-010 · Licensing Standard

> **Summary:** Which licence every piece of this project carries, how it is
> declared, and the two gates that must be passed before anything is published.
> **Epistemic:** Why code, assets and documents are licensed differently, and
> what each choice commits the project to.
> **Pragmatic:** You can license a new file, or check an existing one, without
> a legal consultation.
> **Audience:** Agents · Oracles


## 1. Purpose and scope

`CAN-005` says **why** Numen Games licenses as it does: every piece is born in the
most closed regime that makes sense and is opened by deliberate acts, because
opening is irreversible. That reasoning is canon and stays there.

This standard says **what must be done** about it. It governs the repositories of
Numen Games, including Numinia's and NWOS's. It does not govern the client
workspaces generated by our tools, which are covered by the generator rule below.

Obligations use RFC 2119. Where this standard and `CAN-005` disagree, the canon
rules — but the canon states principle, so a disagreement means one of the two is
wrong and must be corrected, not reconciled.

> This is not legal advice. It is the internal norm and its technical implementation.

---

## 2. Which licence each piece gets

Four questions, in order. The first one that stops the flow wins.

**1 · Is it ours?** Without demonstrable ownership — employment contract,
commission, written assignment or a compatible licence of origin — **it is not
published**.

**2 · Is it executable or is it content?** A shader is code. A `.blend` is content.

**3 · Does it decide or does it show?** If it decides with authority, it is core.
If it shows, computes or describes, it is edge.

**4 · Can it be undone?** If not, the decision is signed by an Oracle — see the
two gates below.

**The repository is not a legal unit.** The licence is determined by what is
distributed or served, not by where the file lives. A monorepo MAY contain
`apps/*` under AGPL and `packages/*` under MIT, declared by `REUSE.toml`. What
MUST be guaranteed is the **direction of the dependencies**: MIT imported by AGPL
is correct — and the MIT package published separately remains MIT; AGPL imported
by MIT is forbidden and a lint rule prevents it.

**Separate repository only for inherited copyleft.** A third party's copyleft can
be enforced against us and its derivative-work boundary is debatable: it is
isolated. Our own copyleft carries no such risk, because we are the owners:
declaring it per directory suffices.

### Software branch

| Case | Licence |
|---|---|
| Depends on a copyleft engine **inherited from a third party** | the engine's, in a **separate repository** |
| Deployable application: platform, backend or service that **decides** (`apps/*`) | `AGPL-3.0-only` |
| Library, package, SDK, types, tokens, script, CI, infrastructure (`packages/*`) | `MIT` |
| Unpublished prototype or spike | reserved until decided |

### Culture branch

| Case | Licence |
|---|---|
| Public catalog: GLB, VRM, textures, sprites, audio, video | `CC0-1.0` |
| Data, metadata, indexes | `CC0-1.0` |
| Design system: tokens, CSS, own iconography | `CC0-1.0` |
| Documentation, ADRs, specifications | `CC-BY-4.0` |
| Lore, narrative, scripts, brand | reserved |
| Third-party material | its licence of origin, without exception |

**The `canon/` corpus is `CC0-1.0`**, by the irrevocable grant recorded in
`CAN-005` and `ADR-036`. It is not an exception to be reasoned about again; it is
a fact about what was already published.

### 2.5 Templates, generators and generated work

**A generator never propagates its licence to what it generates.** Ownership of
the work follows whoever commissions it, not the tool that produces it. This
applies to repository templates, scaffolding, document generators and to NWOS.

- The **template** is a piece of ours and is licensed by the tables above:
  usually `MIT`.
- The **generated work** belongs to the client. The template **MUST NOT** emit its
  own licence into the artifact.
- The template **MUST** emit into the artifact a **reserved-rights `LICENSE` in
  the client's name** — not its own, and not none either, because silence does not
  declare.
- Client workspaces are **not** subject to the declaration rules of this
  standard: their regime is set by the client.

### 2.6 AI-generated content

In several jurisdictions purely machine-generated work **generates no copyright**,
so there may be no ownership to assign or to claim.

- Every piece MUST declare its provenance: `human`, `ai-assisted` or
  `ai-generated`.
- Whatever is delivered or published **as our own work** MUST have substantial,
  recorded human intervention; pure `ai-generated` is declared nobody's work.
- **[LAWYER]** What ownership a contract promises over documents drafted by a
  model is a contractual question, not this standard's, and it precedes billing
  for them.

### 2.7 Before applying CC0 to media

The following MUST be verified: origin of every sample in audio; written consent
if identifiable people appear; EXIF cleanup in photography; complete chain of
rights in video; consent contemplating cloning in voice. **CC0 is not applied to
what contains people without documented consent** — CC0 waives our own rights, it
cannot waive someone else's.

## 3. What we can use

| | Licences |
|---|---|
| **Freely** | `MIT` `ISC` `BSD-2-Clause` `BSD-3-Clause` `Apache-2.0` `0BSD` `CC0-1.0` `CC-BY-4.0` · `OFL-1.1` fonts only |
| **With isolation** | `MPL-2.0` `EPL-2.0` `LGPL-3.0` |
| **With a signed decision, isolated and declared** | `GPL-3.0` `AGPL-3.0` — separate repository if a third party's |
| **Never** | `BUSL` `SSPL` `Elastic` Commons Clause · proprietary · `CC-BY-NC-*` `CC-BY-ND-*` · any dependency without a `license` field |

**Present is not distributed.** The *never* list applies to what is **distributed
or served**. A forbidden transitive dependency that stays out of the final
artifact through tree-shaking **MUST NOT** block development, but **MUST**: be
logged in a `debt/` entry tagged `legal` (`ADR-026`) with an **exit threshold** —
not a date, the condition that makes it untenable; be protected by a CI guard that
inspects the artifact's **contents**, not the tree's names, because bundlers
inline code; and be verified against the real text of the licence, not against its
label. Mandatory check before granting the exception: **if that licence imposes
additional restrictions and the artifact is AGPL, no exception is possible** — it
would be a state impossible to comply with, not merely undesirable.

**Missing `license` field in a transitive dependency.** It is a hygiene signal,
not an autonomous breach: it blocks while the terms are unknown and clears once
they are documented by reading the package's `LICENSE`. What is forbidden is
depending on terms nobody has read.

**Floor rule.** The strongest copyleft in the distributed tree sets the minimum of
the outbound licence: a single GPL `import` excludes MIT as output.
`devDependencies` and build tools do not count; what is bundled into the client
does.

**Direction of pull.** `MIT / BSD / ISC → Apache-2.0 → GPL-3.0 → AGPL-3.0`, and
never the other way.

**The process boundary is the licence boundary.** Two programs talking over HTTP
are two works; two modules importing each other are one.

Every dependency's SPDX is resolved **against the registry before adding it**,
never from memory.

## 4. The two gates

`CAN-005` states why these are irreversible. These are the checks that MUST pass
before the irreversible act.

**Permanent-publication gate (MUST).** Before writing to Arweave, ownership,
absence of incompatible third-party material and absence of personal data are
verified, and an Oracle signs. What does not pass is served only from CDN, which
can be withdrawn.

**Visibility-change gate (MUST).** Turning a repository from private to public
**is the grant**: a licence offer made publicly with the work available grants
rights to whoever takes them, with no need for `npm publish` or Arweave. It is
irreversible in practice and therefore an act signed by an Oracle, with the same
gate as permanent publication. Before changing visibility, verify:

1. `LICENSES/`, `REUSE.toml`, `TRADEMARKS.md` and `NOTICE` complete and correct.
2. **A real listing of sensitive directories against the annotations**, not a
   hand-written list: what is enumerated by hand desynchronizes and the reserved
   publishes itself.
3. No reserved file reachable by a general annotation.
4. No personal data or secrets in the history, not just in `HEAD`.

**Birth licence ≠ publication.** A repository is born with its `LICENSE` from the
first commit; while it is private it grants permission to no one.

## 5. How it is declared

Every public repository MUST contain `LICENSE`, `LICENSES/` with the text of each
licence used, `REUSE.toml`, `TRADEMARKS.md`, and `NOTICE` if it distributes any
Apache-2.0 dependency. Every `package.json` MUST declare `license` with the exact
SPDX.

```ts
// SPDX-FileCopyrightText: 2026 Numen Games S.L.
// SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT
```

```toml
version = 1
[[annotations]]
path = "assets/**"
SPDX-FileCopyrightText = "2026 Numen Games S.L."
SPDX-License-Identifier = "CC0-1.0"
```

**One file, one regime (MUST).** An SPDX expression with `AND` means the recipient
must comply with **both licences over the whole file**, not "this part yes and this
one no". Combining an open licence with reservation that way produces a permission
impossible to comply with: the same defect for which we reject licences that
impose additional restrictions on copyleft artifacts, only self-inflicted.

When two regimes coexist in one file, **the content is separated**: the data, the
lore or the configuration move out to their own file with their own annotation,
and the code stays clean. It is, besides, what File Over App (`STD-006`) already demanded —
lore embedded in a component violates that principle regardless of licences. If
for a technical reason it cannot be separated, REUSE admits
`SPDX-SnippetBegin`/`SnippetEnd` for the fragment; that is the patch, not the
solution.

**Exception — files that cannot be modified.** Third-party kits pinned by
byte-for-byte identity proof, vendored code, generated artifacts and binaries with
no metadata field **MUST NOT** be altered to insert the header: they are declared
via `REUSE.toml` or an adjacent `.license` file. Modifying a pinned file to comply
with this norm is breaking another.

**The licence travels inside the file.** A binary leaving the CDN loses the
README. If the licence does not go inside, it ceases to exist.

| Format | Field |
|---|---|
| glTF / GLB | `asset.copyright` |
| VRM | `VRMC_vrm.meta` — `licenseUrl`, `otherLicenseUrl`, permissions |
| MP3 | ID3v2 `TCOP` + `TXXX:LICENSE` |
| WAV / FLAC / OGG | Vorbis comments `LICENSE`, `COPYRIGHT` |
| JPEG / PNG / WebP | XMP `xmpRights:WebStatement` + `cc:license`; EXIF `Copyright` |
| MP4 / MOV | embedded XMP |
| SVG | RDF with `dc:rights` and `cc:license` |

**VRM (MUST).** The specification's defaults are restrictive and would block a
file declared CC0. Mandatory: `otherLicenseUrl` to CC0, `avatarPermission:
everyone`, `commercialUsage: corporation`, `creditNotation: unnecessary`,
`allowRedistribution: true`, `modification: allowModificationRedistribution`.

**Language of enforceable artifacts (MUST).** `LICENSE`, `LICENSES/`,
`TRADEMARKS.md`, `NOTICE`, the CLA and the agents' fragment go **in English**.
They are what is held against a third party. The original es-ES exception was
revoked on 2026-08-27 (`ADR-023`).

**CI (MUST).** `license-check` fails on an unknown, forbidden or missing licence.
**Default regime: error.** Warning is the exception and requires an explicit list:
only tracks that neither deploy to production nor publish. Severity follows
**exposure**, not the directory's or domain's name; if the system cannot determine
exposure, error.

**Debt thresholds (MUST).** The exits recorded in `debt/` entries tagged `legal`
(`ADR-026`) are expressed as a condition, not a date, and **the CI guard evaluates
them on every build**. The condition is the trigger: a threshold nobody measures
is not an exit, it is a wish.

## 6. Contributions

| Where | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Every repository containing AGPL code, even in one part | **CLA** Apache-style: the contributor keeps their copyright and grants us a broad licence with sublicensing and patents |
| Exclusively MIT repositories and documentation | **DCO**: sign with `git commit -s` |
| Assets | Explicit CC0 declaration in the PR |

The CLA is required **per repository, not per path**. A CLA conditioned on which
files each PR touches is confusing for contributors and its failure is silent: a
contribution to AGPL code accepted without a CLA removes the dual licence over
that file forever, without anyone noticing.

## 7. Fragment for `CLAUDE.md`

Every repository carries a short licensing block in its `CLAUDE.md` so an agent
reads the regime without opening this standard.

**The block is not reproduced here.** This document is its source; keeping a
second copy inside the source is how the two drift apart, and a copy that
contradicts its own origin is worse than no copy. The canonical wording lives
in this repository's `CLAUDE.md`, between the `REUSE-IgnoreStart` and
`REUSE-IgnoreEnd` markers. New repositories copy it from there.

If a repository's block and this standard disagree, this standard rules.

## 8. Conformance

| Obligation | Verified by |
|---|---|
| Every directory declares the licence of what it holds | `check-license-frontmatter.mjs` (`LIC-058`) |
| The licence in a document and the licence in the manifest agree | `check-license-frontmatter.mjs` (`LIC-059`) |
| A permanent publication is gated on a review of ownership | `[MANUAL]` — recorded outside the corpus (`LIC-060`) |
| Dependency SPDX resolved before adding | `[MANUAL]` — no guard exists |
| Direction of dependencies: AGPL MUST NOT be imported by MIT | `[MANUAL]` — the lint rule this standard describes is **not implemented** |
| Floor rule respected in the distributed tree | `[MANUAL]` — no guard inspects bundle contents |
| Visibility-change gate | `[MANUAL]` — Oracle's signature |

**Five of seven are manual.** The two automated ones check that a declaration
exists and is consistent; nothing checks that the declaration is *correct*.
This standard cannot be ratified as enforced — it is enforced by reading.

---

## 9. What this standard does NOT do

It does not state **why** the regimes are what they are, why opening is
irreversible, or why the trademark sits outside every free licence. That is
`CAN-005` and it stays there.

It does not govern the inherited inventory — see `debt/` entries tagged `legal`
(`ADR-026`) — nor the alternatives that were considered and rejected, which are in
the archived Legal Book v0.6.1.

It is not legal advice.
