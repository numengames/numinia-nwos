## What closed

### The glossary exists — `S-001`, merged, unsigned

The archive had no description of its own filing system: 246 documents, 60+
frontmatter fields, nothing written about what any of them meant. `S-001` now
defines the series, the registration scheme, the controlled vocabularies and
the change thresholds.

It went through **four Oracle reviews** and came out at v2.4.0. It is merged and
**deliberately not signed** — see *What stays open*.

### Twelve `cancelled` missions became `frozen`

The five-state vocabulary left 12 missions outside it. Oracle ruling: `frozen` +
`freeze_reason: cancelled`, on the argument that **a withdrawn promise is still
evidence** — deleting them would erase that they were proposed and dropped.

Executed with `scripts/cancel_to_frozen.py`, idempotent, verified: `cancelled`
absent from the corpus, `frozen` reads 13. Filed as `D-016`, **RESOLVED with its
trace** — `debt/` is append-only, and a closed entry is worth more than one that
never existed.

### The README was rewritten — it had drifted four months

Eight findings, each verified before being written down: the repository was
named `numinia-digital-agents` in its own title; the link justifying the repo's
language was broken; **`web/`, the folder that serves numinia.org, was not
mentioned at all**; it taught `cancelled` and `backlog`, both withdrawn; and it
propagated `Functional Model`, retired from canon four months earlier.

**The entry point of the archive was a vector for `D-012`.** Registered as
`D-015`, whose closing condition is not "fix the README" — that is done — but
**a machine that catches the next drift**.

Recorded because they are the session's real content, and because an archive
that only records its successes is `D-012` waiting to happen.

- **The `type` map is not a judgement.** A refactor was proposed that would have
  moved three documents on the strength of their `type`. The first instinct had
  been the opposite error — rewriting the `type` to match the folder, which
  `S-001` §3 forbids. **Both were wrong, in opposite directions.** §3 now
  requires verifying the `type` against the document first.
- **`[PENDING]` was invented.** A third enforcement marker appeared in nine
  places, next to `[CI]` and `[MANUAL]`. It was comfortable: it said "decided,
  just not applied yet" and sounded like progress. `[MANUAL]` forces the
  admission that a rule is not enforcing itself.
- **The HTML claimed to be generated and was not.** It had already drifted:
  renumbered sections, and §9 dropped entirely. `render-glossary.py` exists
  because of that.
- **An auditability gap was filed as an environment quirk.** `D-019`.
- **Immutability was asserted and was false.** 14 of 14 canon documents carry
  more than one commit.

## Addendum — what happened after this report was merged

This report was written and merged at 22:18 while the session was still
running. What follows is the two hours after it, added rather than silently
folded in: **a session report that quietly grows to cover its own aftermath is
the same defect as a hand-written date.**

### `D-021` — the day's best finding, and it is about this agent

Ten documents written during the session declare a `created` date their own
commits contradict. Several are the `debt/` entries defining the rule against
exactly that.

**The count is not the finding.** The Oracle's reading:

> *"What makes it good is not the ten dates: the sequence was coherent fiction.
> A plausible minute after another, crossing midnight, when the six files came
> out of one commit at 22:07. A future reader does not catch that — they take
> it as read."*

A wrong date that looks arbitrary invites suspicion. A wrong date that looks
like a timeline does not. And `D-007` — the entry about `week` being ambiguous
without a year — is one of the ten.

The generalisation, which is why this outranks the dates: **a new rule does not
apply itself to whoever writes it.** `[MANUAL]` is not a weaker `[CI]`; it
means nothing enforces this, and the proof is its own author failing it in the
hour it was written.

`D-021` carries `created_source: git:84e54a4` and `created_confidence: exact` —
the first document in the archive whose date was taken from its commit rather
than typed.

### `MIS-109` inherits the three-field date rule

Ruled and written down before it could be forgotten: the 121 `T00:00:00Z`
documents are corrected with `created` + `created_source` +
`created_confidence`, never with the first alone.

> Fill `created` without declaring which commit it came from and we have
> swapped an invented date for a better-disguised one.

`created_confidence: inferred` is mandatory wherever the history crosses
`MIS-066`'s bulk rename. **An inferred date declared as inferred is evidence;
an inferred date declared as exact is a lie with a citation.**

### `S-001` §9.1 — citing versus mentioning as data

The reference lint, run against `main`, reported `D-018` as broken: it reads the
`RPT-07` row of that entry's inventory table as a live citation.

> A report about broken citations is itself full of broken citations. That is
> what it is for.

Not a bug — a category the lint lacks. Ruled as a **format convention** rather
than a per-document exemption, because an exemption is forgotten by the next
report and a convention is inherited by it. Identifiers mentioned as data are
written in code spans; both linters will skip them.

**An ADR on who may sign what, and how many are needed.** Four Oracles plus
